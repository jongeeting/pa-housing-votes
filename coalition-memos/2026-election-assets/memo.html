Statewide: a Democratic trifecta

Democrats expanded their state House majority and flipped the state Senate for the first time since 1994, while Gov. Shapiro won re-election. For housing reform, control of the Senate is the whole story: it is the chamber where House-passed zoning bills went to die.

Bottom line

The House never killed these bills — HB 2186 passed 139–62 and HB 2109 passed 123–78. They died in a Republican Senate. A Democratic Senate, House, and Governor turns "passes the House, dies in the Senate" into a bill that can actually reach Shapiro's desk. The cost: the bipartisan Republican bench thinned — two of the reform coalition's most reliable Republicans lost (see p. 3).

Where the seats changed hands

Nearly every flip is in the southeast suburban collar — our softest, most-gettable Republican tier — plus the Lehigh Valley. The deep-red rural regions did not move. No opponents were converted; the gains came from suburban seats changing party outright.

New gavels: who chairs the housing & transportation committees

Because the Senate flipped, its current Democratic "minority chairs" take the gavel. The House was already Democratic, so those chairs are unchanged. The highest-leverage change for housing: Nikil Saval — a leading pro-housing voice — would chair Senate Urban Affairs & Housing, the committee that gated these bills.

What to watch

  • The 2 uncalled Senate races (SD-16, SD-40) decide whether the Senate committee losses are one Republican (Farry) or three (Farry, Coleman, Brown) — and firm up the majority.
  • HD-12 (the one Democratic House seat that could still flip R) sets whether the House net is +5 or +4.
  • Re-target the Senate nesting map. Our prior targets — Ward (SD-30), Langerholc (SD-35), Picozzi (SD-5) — all survived; the chamber turned on other seats, so targeting logic changes under Democratic control.
