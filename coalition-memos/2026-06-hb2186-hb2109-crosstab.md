## Top-line crosstab

| Bucket | n | R | D |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yea on BOTH (rock-solid) | 111 | **14** | 97 |
| Nay on BOTH | 28 | 27 | 1 |
| ADU Nay → GG Yea (new R gettables) | 34 | **34** | 0 |
| ADU Yea → GG Nay (slipped) | 28 | **24** | 4 |

R Yea jumped from 38 → 48 (net +10), but underneath there's huge
churn: only 14 of the 38 ADU-Yea Rs held Yea on HB 2109. 24 slipped;
34 new Rs came in.

**Important caveat.** HB 2109 was amended on the floor by A03389
(per-municipality 5-person occupancy cap on unrelated adults, with
student-housing and state-licensed-facility carve-outs). That
amendment narrows the original bill. Many of the 34 "newly-gettable"
Rs may be voting for the *amended* bill — which now gives munis a
5-cap floor — rather than for occupancy reform writ large. Same
explanation likely drives the 4 D defections: Inglis (the ADU
sponsor), Pielli, Vitali, Probst all voted Nay protesting the
amendment.

So: don't over-read this as "occupancy is broader-coalition than
ADUs." It might just be "HB 2109 as amended is a different bill
than ADUs is, and a different bill than HB 2109-as-introduced was."

### Add a "Senate-House nesting math" section to the public memo

(Hold for HB 2109 Final Passage, then build this as a new section
of the political-geography memo — the current public memo doesn't
get into nesting math or Senate targeting based on it.)

Each PA Senate district contains roughly four House districts.
For SB 1346, SB 1239, SB 1281 (and any future Senate zoning bill),
the House votes inside a Senator's nest are a strong gettability
signal. Section should produce:

1. **Nest-vote score per Senator** — for each of the 50 SDs, tally
   how the 3–5 HDs inside it voted on HB 2186 Final Passage and
   HB 2109 Final Passage. "Nest score" = share of nested House
   members voting Yea (weighted by population if cleaner).
2. **Currently-not-on-the-bill Senators with strong nests** —
   intersect against the SB 1346 + SB 1239 + SB 1281 cosponsor
   lists. Surface Senators NOT cosponsoring whose nest scored
   ≥0.5 Yea. Those are the Senate gettables the House votes
   already justify asking for.
3. **Currently-on-the-bill Senators with weak nests** — inverse:
   Senators cosponsoring whose nest scored low. Anchor allies
   we should protect / arm with arguments for their House
   members' positions.
4. **Map**: PA Senate districts shaded by nest score (light → dark
   green for Yea share). Overlay markers for current cosponsors.
5. **Reverse case** — for any House gettables we identified above
   whose Senator IS already on a Senate zoning bill, that's a
   "your own Senator already supports this" frame for outreach
   to the House member.

Inputs needed when we build this:
- `public/data/pa_senate_districts.geojson` (already in repo)
- A House-district → Senate-district nesting table. We don't
  have this yet; build it via spatial join of the two GeoJSONs
  (or by walking each HD's population centroid into containing
  SD). Output as `pipeline/data/hd_to_sd.json`.
- HB 2186 Final Passage + HB 2109 Final Passage roll calls
  (already in `pipeline/data/rollcalls/`).
- Current cosponsor lists for SB 1346, SB 1239, SB 1281 (already
  in `src/data/cosponsors/`).

Caveats to flag in the public memo:
- Nest scores are correlational, not predictive. A Senator can
  diverge from their House caucus for reasons that aren't
  visible in roll-call data (industry ties, leadership posture,
  primary considerations).
- Some HDs straddle SD boundaries; pick a single dominant SD
  per HD by population share rather than splitting votes.
- HB 2109's A03389 amendment confounds reading it as a clean
  zoning signal — should be footnoted in the nest-score
  methodology.
