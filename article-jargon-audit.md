## Opening section (through "Voters' intense single-minded focus")

### "accessory dwellings"
**First mention:** "The two bills — which would allow homeowners to build accessory dwellings in residential neighborhoods…"

**Add light definition + link on first use:**

> The two bills — which would allow homeowners to build **[accessory dwelling units](https://bpnguide.netlify.app/)** (small secondary homes on the same lot, sometimes called in-law suites, granny flats, or carriage houses) in residential neighborhoods, and limit municipal powers to restrict the number of unrelated residents living together — represent a sea change in housing politics inside the Capitol.

*(Link destination: the ADU term in the BPN Guide glossary.)*

### "limit municipal powers to restrict the number of unrelated residents"
Consider a parenthetical — Roxanne's readers may not know this is a real rule.

> "…limit **municipal powers to restrict the number of unrelated residents living together** (currently, many Pennsylvania towns cap how many unrelated people can share a single house — often at three or four)…"

### "state zoning standards bills"
Jargon in paragraph 3. Soften:

> "State lawmakers have grown increasingly comfortable with the idea of **state zoning standards bills — legislation that sets minimum zoning requirements every municipality must follow**, a necessary precondition for…"

### "municipal zoning and permitting processes"
Mostly self-explanatory, but tightening here helps later jargon feel lighter:

> "Everyone* generally ignored the **local zoning and permitting rules — what each town or city decides it will allow built where and how** — that determine whether housing is actually built or not."

## "Why Philly Zoning Politics Needs a State Rescue" section

This section is jargon-heavy for a general audience. Several opportunities to link and define lightly:

### "Councilmanic Prerogative"
Definitely define — most Citizen readers know, but some don't:

> "This is particularly important for Philadelphia where, because of **[Councilmanic Prerogative](https://bpnguide.netlify.app/) — the unwritten but ironclad rule that the Council district member gets the final word on any land-use decision within their district** — it's become almost impossible in practice to pass citywide housing laws anymore."

*(The BPN Guide has a dedicated Councilmanic Prerogative entry under "Political Bodies & Processes.")*

### "zoning overlay districts"
The term "overlay" gets used eight-ish times in this section without ever being defined. First use should include a plain-English definition:

> "The way Council approaches **[zoning overlay districts](https://bpnguide.netlify.app/)** — the neighborhood-specific zoning layers that sit on top of the citywide base zoning code and change the rules for that particular area — is not normal relative to the rest of the state."

### "density bonuses"
Brief parenthetical:

> "The usual idea with an overlay is to create **density bonuses** (rules that let builders put up more housing units if they meet some public-interest condition, like including affordable homes) or other additive incentives to achieve a policy goal…"

### "RSA-5" / "rowhouse code"
If you mention RSA-5 by name, link it. If you don't, you can stay colloquial:

**Option A — stay colloquial (recommended for Citizen readers):**
> "…under the section of the code for rowhouses — Philly's most ubiquitous housing type…"

**Option B — name the district:**
> "…under **[RSA-5](https://bpnguide.netlify.app/philly-zoning-districts.html#RSA-5)**, the zoning district that governs most of Philadelphia's rowhouse neighborhoods…"

I'd stick with Option A. RSA-5 is not a term the average reader needs. The "13 different sets of dimensional rules" line lands harder without forcing them to absorb the code name.

### "dimensional rules"
Light definition:

> "…there are now at least 13 different sets of **dimensional rules — the numbers governing how tall a building can be, how close to the property line, how much of the lot it can cover, and so on** — for how to build a rowhouse…"

### "by-right"
Used in "accessory dwellings will be broadly legal by-right". Link it:

> "…accessory dwellings will be broadly legal **[by-right](https://bpnguide.netlify.app/)** — meaning an owner can build one without needing special permission from the Zoning Board — all across Philadelphia…"

*(BPN Guide has "By-Right Development" under "Zoning & Development Process Terms.")*

### "TOD" / "mixed-use and multifamily" / "off-street parking and use regulations"
These come in a quick list; parentheticals let you keep the pace:

> "Other bills badly needed in Philly and now under consideration at the state level, or slated to be, include a **transit-oriented development (TOD) law** (making it easier to build housing near train, bus, and trolley stations); **a law allowing mixed-use and multifamily housing on commercially-zoned sites** like malls and office parks and downtowns; and laws reforming **off-street parking mandates** (rules that require a minimum number of parking spots for every new home or business) **and use regulations** (rules dictating what kinds of buildings or businesses can exist on a given lot)."

## "Unfortunately, while the Inglis bill package intends…" paragraph

### "Home Rule cities"
Readers will stall on this. Define inline:

> "…while the Inglis bill package intends** to include Philadelphia and Pittsburgh — the two **Home Rule cities** (Pennsylvania cities that write their own charters and are governed by their own locally-passed laws rather than the state's standard municipal code) not regulated by PA's Planning Code like the other municipalities — the Senate looks to be headed in a different direction."

### "Municipalities Planning Code" / "Title 53"
This is the crux of your ** footnote and it comes up in the text. Define them the first time they appear together:

> "To accomplish this, the bills would amend the **Municipalities Planning Code** — the state law that governs zoning and planning for most Pennsylvania towns but *not* Philadelphia and Pittsburgh — instead of **Title 53** of the PA Consolidated Statutes, which applies more broadly."

### "Cities of the First and Second Class"
Already parenthetically defined if you've done the above. In your footnote, consider adding:

> "(In Pennsylvania legal shorthand, 'First Class City' means Philadelphia and 'Second Class City' means Pittsburgh.)"

## Meta-note: one consistent gesture

Roxanne will like it if every jargon term has **the same treatment the first time** — a parenthetical in plain English, or a link, or ideally both. Pick a house style and stick to it. Example:

> "**accessory dwelling units** (small secondary homes on the same lot — sometimes called in-law suites or granny flats)"

vs.

> "**[accessory dwelling units](https://bpnguide.netlify.app/)**"

The first works for print and online; the second only works online. For a Citizen piece that may get picked up elsewhere or excerpted, I'd lean **parenthetical-first, link-second**: define inline, then make the term a link for readers who want the deep dive.
